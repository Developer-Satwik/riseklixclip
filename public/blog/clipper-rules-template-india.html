Clipper Rules Template for Indian Campaigns

Write clear rules for sources, edits, publishing, review and payment before asking clippers to start.

Quick answer

Clipper rules should explain which content is allowed, what to deliver, where to publish and how submissions are reviewed and paid. Include deadlines, corrections, caps, fees and a route for questions. Use the blank template to record your own terms. A draft becomes useful only when the campaign team reviews and agrees it.

A few terms, explained
Disclosure
A clear label telling viewers about an advertising or paid relationship where required. It does not replace permission to use the content.
Payment cap
An agreed limit on spending or rewards. Check what the limit applies to and what happens when it is reached.
Source content
The original video, recording or other material used to make the clip. Check permission, context and any restrictions before editing or publishing.
Campaign brief
The written plan explaining what to make, who it is for, allowed sources, deadlines, review and payment rules.

01

Use rules that describe the actual campaign

A useful rules document translates the brief into actions participants can follow. It should identify the campaign, owner, current version and the route for questions. Rules copied from another project may refer to sources, payment conditions or platforms that do not apply to your work.

Treat the clauses below as an editorial drafting aid. Replace the descriptive instructions with the actual approved terms and review them with the people responsible for the engagement. They are not a completed contract or legal advice. Participants should be able to distinguish a proposed draft from the rules that currently govern their submissions.

02

Define source and reuse boundaries

State which sources participants may use, where they are located and which moments or assets are restricted. Describe the permitted final output and whether publication on participant accounts is allowed or required. Explain how music, screenshots and third-party material should be cleared.

A draft clause can say: use only the approved source list and retain the meaning of the original statement; refer unresolved permission questions to the named owner before editing or publishing. Then add the actual list and owner. Avoid a broad instruction such as “use any viral content” that leaves source authority and campaign relevance unanswered.

03

Make creative acceptance observable

Specify language, file format, framing, caption accuracy, credit and any required disclosure. Describe the context that must remain and the treatment of claims, demonstrations or sensitive information. Use an authorised example to show what a complete idea looks like.

Explain how acceptance works: who reviews, how reasons are provided and how participants submit a correction. A rule such as “quality must be good” does not tell someone how to improve. A more useful rule identifies a missing source reference, unreadable caption or misleading cut and connects it to the required correction. Keep subjective preferences clearly distinguished from factual errors.

04

Describe submission and revision procedure

Define the submission route and required fields. Include a source reference, final version, brief version and post link where publication is part of the task. Give revisions a clear version label and connect them to the original item. State whether a new creative direction counts as a new deliverable.

Write the actual revision scope and timing agreed for the project. Do not imply unlimited revisions unless that is the real arrangement. Explain how a participant should raise a source problem or deadline issue. A documented question route can prevent silent assumptions from turning into several incompatible drafts.

05

State commercial conditions independently

Specify the actual fixed fee, outcome formula or hybrid terms where applicable. Include eligible metric and window, caps, deductions, approval conditions and payment schedule. Explain what happens to work already submitted if the campaign pauses or changes. Use the approved agreement rather than inventing terms to complete the template.

Keep submitted, approved, calculated, scheduled and received states distinct. Explain the official support route for a disputed review or payment. A participant should not have to infer a payable amount from a public counter or a message celebrating views. If a commercial detail is unresolved, leave the draft visibly incomplete and settle it before work begins.

06

Add conduct, disclosure and change handling

Include respectful communication, protection of private information and a clear route for reporting impersonation or suspected manipulation. Require participants to follow the applicable platform and disclosure rules for the actual post. Use the dedicated disclosure guide and current official sources to decide labels and placement.

Give the rules a version and explain how changes are announced. Preserve the terms that applied to existing work and make the effective date of a new instruction clear. Before launch, ask a participant to explain the source, deliverable, acceptance and payment rules back to you. Any ambiguity discovered in that exercise is a drafting problem to fix.

07

Turn each rule into an actionable clause

A useful rule describes the requirement, responsible person, supporting evidence and next action when something is unclear. Replace broad phrases such as “quality content only” with criteria connected to the actual brief. Keep commercial conditions in the appropriate agreement and link the relevant process accurately.

For a new campaign, use placeholders until the owner supplies real details. A sample rule set should be labelled DEMONSTRATION. Rules do not create source permission or establish a payment obligation by themselves; those need their actual supporting arrangements.

08

Find contradictions before publishing the rules

Read the brief, rules and commercial terms together. Look for inconsistent output requirements, review boundaries or evidence definitions. A rule requiring public post links conflicts with a file-only assignment unless the actual scope is changed.

In a fictional check, one document asks for drafts while another describes every submission as already approved. Correct that language before work begins. Contradictions create avoidable disputes because participants cannot know which instruction controls. Record the resolved version and who approved it.

09

Change rules with a clear effective boundary

Identify the change, why it is needed and which assignments it affects. Keep the previous version available where necessary for existing commitments. Do not quietly apply a new acceptance requirement to already submitted work without addressing the actual scope and agreement.

Use a clear announcement route and ask operators to update connected templates. If a material change requires a new decision, record that decision rather than assuming silence is agreement. The version history should make the applicable requirements inspectable.

10

Handle exceptions consistently

Record the issue, relevant rule, evidence and actual decision owner. Explain whether the item needs correction, clarification or a separate scope decision. Avoid making a public example of a participant when a private operational review is appropriate.

Inspect repeated exceptions for weak instructions. If many people misunderstand the same field, improve the rule or onboarding. Keep actual decisions traceable and avoid presenting moderator discretion as proof that contractual or source requirements have been satisfied.

Questions about Clipper Rules Template for Indian Campaigns

Can I paste this as a finished agreement?＋

No. Adapt it to actual approved terms and obtain the review appropriate to the engagement.

Should payment rules be inside the creative checklist?＋

Keep them easy to find and explicit; creative acceptance and payment conditions need separate explanations.

How should I announce a changed rule?＋

State the version, effective point and impact on work already submitted.

What is the best clarity check?＋

Ask a participant to explain what they may use, what they must deliver and how acceptance and payment work.

Can I reuse a rules template unchanged?＋

Use it as a structure, then fill it with the actual source, scope, owners and processes. Check it against the brief and agreement. Unfilled placeholders and demonstration terms should not appear as live campaign requirements.

What should happen when rules change mid-campaign?＋

Record the change and its effective boundary, identify affected assignments and address scope or agreement implications. Keep applicable earlier versions where needed. Do not silently rewrite the criteria used for existing submissions.
