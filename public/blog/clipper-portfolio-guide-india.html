Clipper Portfolio Guide

Show samples with clear permission, your editing decisions and an honest account of your role.

Quick answer

A useful clipper portfolio shows what you edited, why you chose the moment and how you handled captions and context. Use content you have permission to share. Label practice work and explain your role in team projects. Do not present invented results, public view counts or someone else’s edit as your own evidence.

A few terms, explained
Source content
The original video, recording or other material used to make the clip. Check permission, context and any restrictions before editing or publishing.
Campaign brief
The written plan explaining what to make, who it is for, allowed sources, deadlines, review and payment rules.
01

Show the work a buyer or reviewer needs to assess

A clipper portfolio should demonstrate the skills relevant to the work you want: source selection, accurate captions, coherent pacing, clear framing and reliable handoff. A montage of effects can show visual ability while leaving those practical skills unanswered.

Choose a small set of your strongest permitted examples and explain the purpose of each. The viewer should know what you did, what the source required and why the edit is complete. You do not need fabricated client logos or large performance claims. A clear demonstration with honest context can be more informative than an unexplained screenshot of a public counter.

02

Use authorised samples and label demonstrations

Use material you own, have permission to edit or can lawfully reuse under terms that fit the portfolio use. Confirm whether client work may be shown and whether private information needs removal. Access to a source during an assignment does not automatically grant public portfolio rights.

If you create a practice edit, label it as a demonstration and name your role. Do not imply that the source owner commissioned or approved your service. Fictional data and practice briefs should remain visibly fictional. This distinction lets you show capability while keeping customer projects and real outcomes separate from exercises.

03

Make each example a concise case note

Include the audience question, source reference where permitted, your editing decisions, final deliverable and any important restriction. Explain how you preserved the source’s meaning or made a demonstration easier to follow. Keep the explanation short enough to inspect beside the video.

A hypothetical case note could say that you edited an authorised practice interview into a complete explanation, corrected mixed-language captions and kept the speaker’s qualification. It should not claim a campaign result unless you have the actual evidence and permission to publish it. Describe the work you can substantiate rather than turning every sample into a success story.

04

Include a revision example

A before-and-after pair can show how you respond to feedback when both versions may be shared. Explain the specific correction: restored context, clearer caption grouping, better demonstration crop or a more complete ending. Keep the reason connected to the source and objective.

Do not present a misleading initial cut as an acceptable technique simply because the later version is better. The example should demonstrate review judgement and learning. If a client’s feedback is private, summarise only what you are permitted to share or use a clearly labelled practice exercise instead.

05

Organise the portfolio for quick inspection

Use a simple accessible page or folder with working links, readable labels and mobile-friendly previews. Group work by relevant skill or source type. Check that a reviewer can view the final asset without requesting access to every file or downloading an unnecessarily large archive.

Keep contact or introduction details current and state the services you can actually perform. If you work in particular languages or tools, demonstrate that capability where possible. Avoid describing yourself as experienced in a format that appears nowhere in the work. A focused portfolio makes a realistic first conversation easier for both sides.

06

Maintain the portfolio as your skill changes

After an assignment, ask whether the work can be included and record any restrictions. Replace weaker practice examples when stronger permitted work becomes available. Recheck links and remove outdated or unapproved material. Keep source and permission notes privately even when the public case note is brief.

Use the portfolio to identify your next learning task. If all examples are caption-led interviews, practise a permitted product demonstration. If reviewers repeatedly ask about source context, improve the case notes. The portfolio should become a clearer record of your judgement over time, without inflating experience, income or audience results.

07

Build a case study around an editorial decision

Choose an authorised project or a clearly labelled practice exercise. Explain the source problem, audience question, your role and the decision you made. Show how the cut preserves meaning and what the handoff contains.

A portfolio becomes more informative when a client can see your judgment, not only a montage of polished exports. If you did not publish, review or manage the work, do not imply that you did. Keep any private source, client or participant information within the actual permission for public display.

08

Use a repeatable case-study worksheet

Give each example enough context to be understood quickly. Include the actual constraints and the part of the work you performed. If the sample is a demonstration, say so near the asset and in the description, not only in a remote disclaimer.

The worksheet below can be left blank for new work. Add actual results only when evidence and permission support their use. A source-to-export explanation can demonstrate useful skill without fabricating a client engagement or audience metric.

09

Show process evidence selectively

A permitted transcript excerpt, annotated source boundary or before-and-after caption correction can explain skill. Select evidence that helps the visitor assess your decision. Avoid exposing whole private source libraries or commercial messages to make the example look more substantial.

If you show a revision, explain what changed and why. If you show audience results, identify the actual metric, observation period and limitations, with permission. Do not attribute a client’s wider campaign outcome entirely to your edit when the evidence cannot support that claim.

10

Test the portfolio as a prospective client

Check whether a visitor can understand your role, view the actual work and find the next appropriate contact route on a phone. Inspect labels, playback or file access and concise context. Record actual usability findings rather than inventing visitor feedback.

Keep the portfolio focused on the work you want to do next. A small set of well-explained examples can be more useful than many ambiguous clips. Update outdated scope descriptions and broken links so the portfolio remains an honest account of your current capabilities.

Questions about Clipper Portfolio Guide

Can I include practice edits?＋

Yes, with appropriate source permission and visible demonstration labels.

Do I need a viral result to have a useful portfolio?＋

No. Show clear source selection, accurate editing and an inspectable explanation of your role.

Should I include client work without asking?＋

Confirm permission and any restrictions before publishing it.

What is a good revision example?＋

A permitted before-and-after that explains a specific source, caption or comprehension correction.

Can a beginner use practice work in a portfolio?＋

Yes, with permitted source use and prominent demonstration labels. Explain the audience, role and editorial decision without implying a real client or paid result. Practice work can show skill when its evidence limits are clear.

How much source material should a public case study expose?＋

Only what you are permitted to display and what helps explain the decision. Keep private records and unnecessary source files out of the public example. A concise annotated excerpt can be more useful than an indiscriminate archive.
