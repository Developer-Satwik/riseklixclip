First Campaign Scope Checklist

Agree the goal, sources, deliverables, budget, review and stop conditions before the first batch.

Quick answer

A first campaign scope should explain the audience, goal, permitted sources, deliverables, reviewer and payment rules. Choose a small first batch and a budget limit you can manage. State what happens to pending work if the campaign pauses or the brief changes. Put these decisions in writing before recruiting participants.

A few terms, explained
Attribution
A method of connecting a visit, enquiry or order to a campaign. It can miss activity or overlap with other methods; a connection does not prove the campaign caused the result.
Disclosure
A clear label telling viewers about an advertising or paid relationship where required. It does not replace permission to use the content.
Source content
The original video, recording or other material used to make the clip. Check permission, context and any restrictions before editing or publishing.
Campaign brief
The written plan explaining what to make, who it is for, allowed sources, deadlines, review and payment rules.

01

Define the smallest useful result

A first campaign should answer one meaningful question for the audience and one operating question for the team. You might want to learn whether a podcast archive contains complete short-form stories, or whether a product demonstration can be understood without the full webinar. Start with that useful result rather than a large output promise.

Write a scope sentence naming the source, audience, deliverable and review owner. Add what is excluded so adjacent expectations do not enter the project silently. If the campaign delivers files for owned channels, say so. If it includes participant-account posts, identify that separate responsibility and the evidence required to close it.

02

Check the source and permission package

Confirm the approved recordings, current claims, usable audio and any restrictions. Include source identifiers, timestamps, names and pronunciations that the editor will need. Identify the owner who can resolve a permission or meaning question. A file being available in a shared folder does not settle its permitted reuse.

Select a small set of complete ideas that support the objective. If a source requires substantial restoration, new recording or clarification, include that work explicitly or remove it from the first scope. A source-readiness check can prevent a pilot from becoming an unexpected archive repair project.

03

Check the people and review capacity

Name who writes the brief, edits, reviews, publishes and reports. Confirm that the reviewers can understand the language and subject. Reserve time for corrections and final publication checks where required. A cohort larger than the available review capacity will create a queue rather than a stronger first campaign.

Use a small demonstration draft to estimate the actual effort before assigning the full scope. Keep the demonstration labelled and separate from customer evidence. If the test reveals repeated ambiguity, improve the brief first. Production volume should follow a working handoff instead of being used to compensate for an unclear one.

04

Check acceptance and commercial terms

Define what counts as a final deliverable, which corrections are included and how decisions are communicated. Identify the terms that govern fixed fees or performance rewards, including the payment trigger and timing. Confirm the actual scope with the responsible parties before participants invest work.

Do not fill an unanswered field with an optimistic default. Mark it unresolved and give it an owner. A practical checklist has ready, incomplete and not applicable states, rather than a decorative sequence of ticks. The purpose is to discover missing decisions while they are still easy to fix.

05

Check publication and reporting separately

Confirm the account owner, final caption, credit, disclosure and publication responsibility. If the deliverable ends at file handoff, record that boundary. If a post is required, define which link and evidence complete the submission. Make sure the current approved version reaches the publisher.

For reporting, choose a window and metric that the team can actually observe. Keep audience evidence separate from revenue attribution and payment calculations. The first campaign may teach you more about source quality and review than long-term distribution. That is useful learning when described honestly, rather than turned into an exaggerated success claim.

06

Agree the close and next decision

At the end, reconcile deliverables, review decisions, publication records and outstanding obligations. State what is complete and what still requires action. Save the organised source and final assets under the agreed retention and reuse conditions.

Then make one clear next decision: continue the approach, improve a specific handoff, change the source type or stop the unsuitable scope. Base that decision on observed work and available evidence. A first campaign is successful as an operating experiment when it produces useful assets and a clearer next step, even if it does not establish a repeatable audience outcome yet.

07

Write the boundary in concrete deliverables

State the authorised source, audience question, required files or posts and the person who reviews them. Identify what the owner must still supply. Include revision and handoff boundaries through the actual agreement rather than a vague instruction to manage everything.

For a fictional first campaign, two accepted files for an owned channel is a different boundary from two participant-account posts with evidence. Choose the actual completion definition before discussing scale. This small distinction can prevent most downstream confusion about account access and reporting.

08

Use a readiness gate with honest states

Check whether source permission, usable material, review ownership and applicable terms are actually available. Mark each field ready, pending or unresolved with supporting evidence. A completed-looking checklist should not conceal a missing decision.

Work on independent preparation where useful, but keep dependent actions conditional. A demonstration checklist may illustrate the structure; it must not appear to show a new customer’s real progress. Readiness comes from supplied information and decisions, not prefilled sample statuses.

09

Test how a scope change would be handled

Imagine a hypothetical owner adds a second language after the first draft. Identify the translation, caption rendering and competent review work the change introduces. Record the new requirement and address its effect through the actual agreement.

Do not silently assume the original participant can perform every added task. Clarify whether the source and publication permissions still cover the new use. A change procedure is useful before the first campaign because it gives the team a clear way to handle a normal evolving requirement.

10

Define close before starting

List what proves completion: accepted identifiable files, actual posts if included, required evidence and commercial reconciliation under the agreement. Keep pending revisions and disputed items in their true states. An owner should know what remains after the handoff.

At close, record practical lessons about source quality, review and scope clarity. Use actual observations instead of a fabricated success case. A bounded first campaign can produce useful learning even when audience or business outcomes are uncertain.

Questions about First Campaign Scope Checklist

Does a first campaign need a large cohort?＋

No. Start with source quality and the review capacity available for a useful bounded scope.

Can a checklist have unresolved items?＋

Yes. Make them visible with an owner, and settle essential dependencies before launch.

Should reporting include revenue automatically?＋

Only when the project has an appropriate attribution method and the necessary evidence.

What should I retain at close?＋

The agreed source, brief, final assets, review and reporting records, with outstanding obligations separately identified.

What is the most important first-campaign scope decision?＋

Define whether completion means accepted files, actual published packages or a broader managed handoff. That boundary determines account, review and evidence responsibilities. Support it with the real source and agreement.

Can a first campaign proceed with pending fields?＋

Prepare independent work where useful, but keep dependent steps conditional. Identify the missing decision and its owner. Do not display a planned permission, review or payment event as completed.
