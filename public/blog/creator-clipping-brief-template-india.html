Build a clear campaign brief covering sources, audience, deliverables, reviews and payment conditions.

Quick answer

A clipping brief explains what to make, which sources are allowed, who it is for and how work will be reviewed and paid. Use the blank builder to record your own plan, then copy or download it. Creating a draft does not send an enquiry or start a campaign. Review and share it separately.

A few terms, explained
Disclosure
A clear label telling viewers about an advertising or paid relationship where required. It does not replace permission to use the content.
Source content
The original video, recording or other material used to make the clip. Check permission, context and any restrictions before editing or publishing.
Campaign brief
The written plan explaining what to make, who it is for, allowed sources, deadlines, review and payment rules.

01

Use the template as a decision document

A clipping brief should remove ambiguity before participants edit. It combines the audience objective, approved sources, creative expectations, submission process and commercial conditions. The editable tool on this page starts empty and creates a local draft that you can copy or download. It does not send a lead or confirm a campaign.

Complete the draft with the actual operator and source owner. A downloaded template is not an agreement until the relevant parties confirm its terms. Keep one current version in a read-only location and retain the earlier versions that governed submitted work. A useful brief can be short enough to scan while still answering the questions that change the edit or payment decision.

02

Define the audience and useful outcome

Describe who the clip is for, their language and their familiarity with the topic. State one primary objective: explain a product feature, introduce a podcast idea, support event discovery or generate qualified enquiries. Avoid a broad goal such as “go viral” that gives reviewers no basis for choosing useful source moments.

Add the next step the viewer should take, if one is relevant. Check that the destination exists and fulfils the clip’s promise. A course clip offering a practical lesson should not end with an unrelated urgency claim. When several audiences need different context, create separate creative tracks instead of expecting one generic cut to serve all of them.

03

Specify approved sources and restrictions

List the source links or files, owner, speakers and useful timestamps. Record the permitted platforms, account types and reuse boundaries. Mark private, embargoed or do-not-use moments. Include any audio, guest or third-party restrictions rather than assuming the source recording grants every right.

Add a glossary for names and recurring terms. Identify claims that require approval, such as product capabilities, financial figures or testimonials. If a source is outdated, explain what remains usable and what must be excluded. Give the editor enough surrounding context to preserve meaning. A recommended timestamp is a candidate moment, not permission to remove an essential qualification.

04

Describe deliverables and creative examples

State whether the work ends with files, published posts or both. Name formats, language, caption conventions, cover requirements and any agreed export settings. Define the quantity or budget boundary and what counts as a distinct deliverable. If several editors share a source, assign angles or moments to reduce duplication.

Give one approved example and explain its useful qualities: complete answer, readable text, clear audio and an honest opening. An unacceptable example can also help when it illustrates a specific defect, such as a missing caveat. Avoid making subjective style the only acceptance rule. Participants should know which changes are required and which are creative suggestions.

05

Write review and publication rules

Name the reviewer, support route, deadlines and timezone. State whether approval is required before publication, how revisions are submitted and which version is final. Describe the allowed accounts and how post changes, removal or privacy settings affect the record.

Include the submission fields: campaign ID, brief version, source link and timestamps, final asset or post URL, account, caption, disclosure and publication time where relevant. Keep feedback specific and linked to the original submission. Define a route for appealing a mistaken decision. Clear review rules are as important as creative examples because they determine how work becomes accepted.

06

Separate payment conditions from creative approval

Write the fee or rate, eligible metric, threshold if any, cap, deductions and payment schedule. Identify which event creates the obligation: accepted file, approved post, eligible performance or another defined milestone. Explain what happens when the campaign pauses or the available budget closes.

Do not leave participants to infer those conditions from a headline rate. A clip may be creatively approved but still subject to a separate measurement or payment window. Keep private payout information outside the public brief. Confirm commercial and specialist requirements with the responsible parties before sharing the draft as final.

07

A hypothetical brief excerpt

For a planning demonstration, imagine an approved founder interview intended for Hinglish first-time business owners. The objective is to explain one customer-research lesson. The source record supplies the interview and relevant timestamp, the final cut must retain the limitation in the answer, and the reviewer approves the file before owned-channel publication.

The commercial row would still need actual quantities, deadlines, rates and payment terms from the operator. The example deliberately leaves those undecided. It shows how audience, source and acceptance connect; it is not a live campaign or a claim of available paid work. Use your own confirmed details in the editable draft instead of copying hypothetical figures into a public offer.

08

Review, distribute and version the finished brief

Read the draft from three perspectives: can the editor start, can the reviewer decide and can the administrator calculate the obligation? Ask a participant to identify any missing field before launch. Consolidate corrections into the current version and announce substantive changes.

When a scope changes, identify affected submissions and apply the agreed change process. At close, preserve the final brief with the approval and payment records. Remove expired offers and source access from future materials. The template becomes valuable when it records real decisions and stays current, not merely when every field contains text.

09

An annotated demonstration shows the brief’s purpose

A fictional brief can identify the project as DEMONSTRATION: founder interview, the audience as new users with one workflow question, and the output as complete owned-channel files. The source field remains a placeholder until a real authorised recording is supplied. The example must not look like a customer capture or approved live campaign.

The owner identifies which statement needs product review and which caption convention the audience needs. The editor receives distinct questions rather than a request for many viral clips. The publisher receives only the accepted package. This demonstration explains the relationships between fields; it does not establish permission, rates, availability or a real project result.

10

Treat blank essentials as a readiness decision

A blank field can reveal missing information more honestly than a guessed default. Source permission, final output, acceptance and commercial conditions need settlement before affected work starts. Other fields may be not applicable, but explain why instead of silently omitting them.

Give each unresolved field an owner and next action. For example, the source owner can confirm reuse, the product owner can approve a claim and the campaign owner can settle output scope. The editable tool remains an empty draft for the user’s real project. Do not populate it with demonstration evidence or automatically turn a completed form into a submitted or accepted campaign.

11

Write examples that teach the desired creative decision

A useful example shows the complete idea, audience and source boundary. Explain why the opening fits, what qualification remains and how the ending fulfils the question. A list of fashionable effects does not teach an editor which source moment to choose.

For a fictional task demonstration, the acceptable direction includes a readable starting state, actual steps and a bounded output. An unsuitable direction might use an unrelated successful screen to imply a stronger result. Keep both examples labelled and source-faithful. Use the contrast to clarify the acceptance rule, not to create fake customer evidence or a universal performance promise.

12

Use a change memo when the brief evolves

Record the version, changed field, reason, affected assignments and treatment of existing submissions. Name the approver and effective point. A change memo can be short, but it should tell participants which instruction now applies and whether their current work needs action.

For a fictional scope change from file delivery to participant publication, review accounts, captions, disclosure, evidence and commercial terms rather than adding one sentence to the old brief. Preserve the previous agreement for earlier work. The brief is useful when it governs real decisions and remains coherent through changes, not merely when every box contains text.

Questions about Creator Clipping Brief Template for India

Does creating a draft submit an enquiry?＋

No. The draft stays in your browser. Copy or download it, review the terms and share it through the published contact route when you choose.

Can I download a blank version without JavaScript?＋

The page includes a plain-text blank template link. The interactive draft tool adds browser editing and local storage.

Is the template a legal contract?＋

It is a planning document. Confirm commercial, rights and applicable specialist requirements with the responsible parties before relying on it as an agreement.

What should happen when rules change mid-campaign?＋

Publish a new version, identify affected work and follow the agreed change process. Do not silently replace the terms that governed earlier submissions.

Should I complete every field with a default to make the brief look ready?＋

No. Leave unknown information visibly unresolved and name who can answer it. Essential source, acceptance and commercial conditions need actual decisions. Mark not applicable where appropriate. A complete-looking form is less useful than an honest readiness record.

What makes a creative example helpful?＋

It explains the audience question, complete source boundary and specific acceptance decision. Show why the selected opening and ending work and what important context remains. Keep the example authorised or visibly fictional, and avoid treating a style preference as a guarantee of reach.
