## Search-landscape method and findings

Web discovery queries included “indian clipping campaign”, “indian clipping platform”, “clipping in india”, “clipping marketplace India”, “does clipping work in india”, “how to choose a clipping agency”, copyright and reused-content combinations. These were search-tool discovery results, not a location-controlled Google ranking study. No position, rank movement or market share is inferred.

Provider-owned results included [ClipConnect](https://clipconnect.in/), [Klassely](https://klassely.com/), [Wondeed](https://www.wondeed.com/), [Dashrize](https://dashrize.com/video-clipping-india), [Clipping.net](https://clipping.net/) and agency guides such as [VALORAE Arc](https://www.valoraearc.com/blog/how-to-choose-a-clipping-agency) and [Clipping Culture](https://clippingculture.com/blog/how-to-choose-a-clipping-agency). These establish what sellers describe, not that their payouts, views or customer claims have been independently verified. Some results advertise precise fees, reward rates, minimums or returns; this expansion does not borrow those figures as Indian benchmarks.

The editorial opportunity is to make the decisions behind those claims inspectable: what is being purchased, which permission is needed, how a metric is defined, what evidence supports a reward and what a buyer can check before commissioning work. The new pages provide original worksheets and examples rather than copying vendor lists or making an unsupported “best” ranking.

## What can strengthen AI-source eligibility

[Google’s current generative-AI guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) prioritises useful original coverage and foundational SEO. It does not require an AI-specific schema, special writing formula or `llms.txt`. The work therefore concentrates on clear answers, practical reasoning, verifiable sources, usable pages and contextual discovery links. Article and breadcrumb markup describe the visible page. FAQs serve readers; they do not imply FAQ rich-result eligibility.

The site keeps existing URLs and gives each new article a single canonical URL. All text is rendered in the static HTML, including the longer guidance and FAQs. Resource and collection pages, contextual links and the sitemap expose the new articles. Metadata explains the actual question and resource rather than listing keyword variants. Authorship remains the real organisation, with no invented specialist reviewer.

## Implementation and assessment

All ten selected articles have been individually written and integrated. They add **19,776 reading words**, with **1,842–2,077 per article**, twelve substantive sections and six FAQs each. They include thirty practical tables and twenty-two distinct primary-reference URLs. The complete library contains **103 articles and 114 pages**; all 104 original routes remain. Thirty-three contextual links from established articles connect readers to the new resources. See the [article-by-article record](new-articles-report.md) for titles, routes, distinct purposes and counts.

The priority is coverage of unresolved decisions, not repetition of the top keyword. Feasibility and pricing support the campaign cluster; category distinctions and agency procurement support the platform/service cluster; legitimacy and rights help users assess participation; YouTube contribution and captions improve production quality; tracking and case-study evidence make outcome claims inspectable. Existing core landing pages retain their scope and URLs. No competitor typo pages or unsupported best-platform rankings were added.

Each resource is rendered in static HTML with its own title, description, canonical URL, focused answer, breadcrumbs and accurate reading count. New local drafts use their actual October creation date instead of the original June publication date. The organisation remains the author; no specialist credentials or human legal review are invented. There is no AI-specific schema or assumed citation benefit from the existing informational llms.txt.

The full browser audit passed **114 pages and 103 article bodies** at 1440, 390 and 320 pixels, including unique metadata, original routes, links, headings, images and authored paragraphs. All ten new articles additionally passed short-laptop contents navigation, keyboard access, mobile tables, light/dark modes and no-JavaScript content/FAQs. Testing found and repaired long-URL overflow in the tracking guide. Representative rendered views were visually inspected. Existing menu, theme choice, calculators, blank draft workflows and guide-plus-Discord behaviour remain functional. These are local checks, not field performance or search gains.

The global editorial audit found **zero repeated full body paragraphs** across all 103 articles. This detects exact prose duplication; it does not claim a quantitative search-cannibalisation or semantic similarity study. The topic map and manual drafting keep the new practical jobs distinct, while future page/query data should be used to detect overlapping search intent after an authorised publication.

The main remaining research limits are the unavailable fresh GSC account data, direct retrieval failures for official Indian copyright chapters, vendor self-description without independent product testing, and the absence of real client performance evidence for the teaching examples. These limits are reflected in the articles rather than concealed by stronger claims.
