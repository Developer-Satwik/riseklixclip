## What improves SEO, AEO and GEO here

The work strengthens useful answers, source clarity, crawlable HTML, consistent identity, navigation and genuine tools. Google’s current guidance supports those foundations and says special AI markup or llms.txt is not needed for Google visibility. The existing llms.txt is retained as a simple index, with no ranking claim. Structured data describes the visible page. [Google’s generative AI search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), [Article structured data documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article).

All 104 existing URL paths and production canonicals remain. Titles and descriptions are unique. Article publication dates are preserved from the supplied site; revised articles show 7 October 2026. Policies retain their original effective dates and distinguish a presentation update. No schema claims an invented person, address, review score, client result or product price.

All 93 articles now have two individual depth revisions. Each has a distinct editorial purpose, a focused answer, 10–13 substantive sections and 5–6 specific FAQs. The second pass adds four individually authored practical sections and two FAQ answers to every article. Guidance includes source-selection methods, editing storyboards, language exercises, campaign worksheets, reusable records, troubleshooting and explicitly hypothetical worked examples. Comparable topics have separate jobs: provider comparisons explain responsibility differences, alternative pages plan a transition or pilot, and specialist campaign pages differ from their hands-on editing guides. Contextual source citations and internal reading links accompany the relevant sections. The article-by-article record is in [the second-depth report](deep-expansion-report.md); research evidence and limitations are in [the expansion report](article-expansion-report.md).

Depth follows the question and useful coverage rather than a keyword-density or word-count target. Google’s [helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) supports that approach. Advice, provider descriptions, official platform policy and hypothetical scenarios are distinguished. There are no invented campaign results, salaries, testimonials or forecasts. Updated reading times and Article word counts describe the visible expanded content.

Useful tools replace missing deliverables: editable/downloadable briefs, a real ROI calculator, and a rate-based earnings estimator. All start empty. Tables, formulas, policy links and primary references remain available in HTML; JavaScript adds interaction. Public navigation and article text are available without JavaScript.

Competitor research used the official sites for [Clipping.net](https://clipping.net/), [Whop](https://whop.com/blog/content-rewards/), [Vyro](https://vyro.com/), [MakeClout](https://makeclout.com/) and [ClipConnect](https://clipconnect.in/). Their feature descriptions are labelled provider claims, not audited results. During the expansion check, ClipConnect’s app did not expose readable current commercial terms, so the comparison asks for written confirmation and does not repeat fee claims from the earlier review. MakeClout currently presents Clouted. No advertised earnings were used as an Indian salary benchmark.

Platform/policy references include [YouTube Shorts analytics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en), [ASCI guidelines](https://www.ascionline.in/social/) and [NPCI fraud awareness](https://www.npci.org.in/fraud-awareness). Source links support their relevant platform or policy details, not RiseKlix performance claims.

## Translation of the six supplied design references

| Reference composition | Applied to RiseKlix |
|---|---|
| Egypt / Assassin’s Creed: cinematic depth, fragments and torn transitions | Charcoal figure collage, layered architecture and a torn edge from the dark hero into the reading surface. |
| Caesar / EGO: heroic scale and controlled hierarchy | Oversized homepage headline, chapter numerals and generous spacing. No unrelated historic identity. |
| Taste the Notes: a central stage and purposeful atmosphere | Theatrical scale and depth around an engraved figure; restrained ink accents. |
| Browser History / Good Move: editorial collage and immersive opening | Content-led hero, a tactile composition and a clear next action inside the scene. |
| Aesthetics Harmony: diagonal cutouts and revealed layers | Cut-paper article covers and offset layered frame graphics. |
| 1917: monumental type interacting with a scene | Large outline numerals and headline/artwork depth, with readable body copy. |

Inter remains the sole family. Graphite, white and gray provide the foundation. Blue identifies direct answers/briefs, violet the clipper/earnings tools, and amber ROI planning. Dark mode uses achromatic charcoal layers (`#171717`, `#1e1e1e`, `#262626`) with small colored labels and accents instead of navy or purple panel fills. Control borders remain distinct from decorative dividers. Themes include System, Light and Dark. The artwork stays a creative illustration and is never represented as campaign evidence.

The artwork was revised after owner feedback to a charcoal-and-paper collage. The film-ribbon first pass is archived; the exact prompt and current paths are in `artwork-v2.md`.

After the owner’s mobile/theme feedback, the mobile hero uses a separate in-flow landscape artwork frame below the text and actions. Its right-aligned crop removes quiet negative space while retaining the reaching figure and architecture; no text or gradient covers it. Article covers remain visible on mobile. The original artwork file is unchanged.

Motion is restrained to a very slow desktop hero drift and hover movement of layered frames. The mobile artwork stays still. Reduced-motion preferences disable it. Article and policy layouts use calmer spacing and readable line lengths.
